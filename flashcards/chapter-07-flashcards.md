## Chapter 7: AML/CFT Obligations, VASP Requirements, and the FATF Travel Rule — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | AML vs CFT — core distinction? | AML = combating concealment of proceeds from crime (placement/layering/integration). CFT = combating financing of terrorism, where funds may be entirely legitimate but destined for terrorist use. Exam tip: source of funds (illicit) is AML's focus; end use (terrorism) is CFT's focus. |
| 2 | FATF VASP definition — what four+ activities qualify an entity as a VASP? | Exchange between virtual assets and fiat; exchange between virtual assets; transfer of virtual assets; safekeeping/administration; participation in/provision of financial services related to an issuer's offer/sale of a virtual asset — when conducted as a business on behalf of another person. Exam tip: must be 'on behalf of another' — pure non-custodial software is not automatically a VASP. |
| 3 | What is FATF Recommendation 16? | The 'Travel Rule' — requires originator and beneficiary information to travel with wire/virtual asset transfers. Extended to virtual assets via FATF's 2019 Interpretive Note to R.16. |
| 4 | FATF Travel Rule threshold (international standard)? | USD/EUR 1,000 — full originator/beneficiary info required above this; reduced info set permitted below. Exam tip: don't confuse with the US BSA/FinCEN $3,000 money transmitter threshold. |
| 5 | US FinCEN Travel Rule (Recordkeeping Rule) threshold? | $3,000 under 31 CFR 1010.410 — historically applied to money transmitters/MSBs. Distinct from FATF's international $1,000 guidance figure. |
| 6 | What information must accompany a virtual asset transfer above the Travel Rule threshold? | Originator: name, account/wallet number, physical address or national ID/DOB+POB or customer ID. Beneficiary: name and account/wallet number. Exam tip: below threshold, only originator name, account number, and wallet address are required. |
| 7 | What is the 'sunrise issue'? | The regulatory gap created when jurisdictions adopt and enforce the Travel Rule at different times, so a compliant VASP may transact with a counterparty VASP under no equivalent legal obligation to collect/retain the same data. |
| 8 | Risk-based approach (RBA) — definition? | Allocating AML/CFT compliance resources (EDD, monitoring, controls) proportionally to assessed risk across customer type, product, geography, and delivery channel — rather than uniform controls for all customers. |
| 9 | FinCEN's role in crypto AML/CFT? | US Treasury bureau administering the Bank Secrecy Act (BSA); classifies certain crypto businesses as Money Services Businesses (MSBs), requiring registration, AML programs, SARs, and recordkeeping — domestic implementation, not international standard-setting. |
| 10 | OFAC's role in crypto AML/CFT? | Administers and enforces US sanctions programs (e.g., SDN List); applies strict liability — has directly sanctioned specific wallet addresses. Distinct from FinCEN's BSA/AML recordkeeping mandate. |
| 11 | FATF's role vs FinCEN/OFAC? | FATF is an intergovernmental standard-setting body (soft law, Recommendations, mutual evaluations, grey/black listing) — it has no direct enforcement power in any single country, unlike FinCEN (US regulator) or OFAC (US sanctions enforcer). |
| 12 | Common exam trap: Travel Rule thresholds | Exam often tests confusing FATF's international $1,000 Travel Rule guidance with FinCEN's US-specific $3,000 Recordkeeping Rule threshold for money transmitters — these are NOT the same figure. |

## Chapter 4: Building, Signing, Broadcasting a Transaction, and Wallet Custody Models — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | How many confirmations is a Bitcoin transaction traditionally treated as 'final' by exchanges for large deposits? | 6 confirmations (~60 minutes at a 10-minute average block time). Exam tip: more confirmations lower reorg risk, but no blockchain transaction has absolute mathematical finality. |
| 2 | Three core components of a UTXO-based transaction (e.g., Bitcoin)? | Inputs, Outputs, and Fee. The fee is not a separate field — it's the leftover amount (sum of inputs minus sum of outputs) claimed by the miner. |
| 3 | What is the mempool? | The pool of unconfirmed transactions broadcast to the network, waiting to be picked up by a miner/validator. Exam tip: transactions with higher fee rate (sat/vByte) get prioritized for inclusion. |
| 4 | Hot wallet vs Cold wallet — key difference? | Hot wallet: private keys stored on an internet-connected device (convenient, higher risk). Cold wallet: private keys stored offline, e.g., hardware device or paper (less convenient, higher security). Exam tip: exchanges keep the bulk of customer funds in cold storage. |
| 5 | Custodial vs Non-custodial wallet — who controls the keys? | Custodial: a third party (e.g., exchange) holds the private keys on your behalf. Non-custodial: the user holds their own private keys/seed phrase. Exam tip: 'Not your keys, not your coins.' |
| 6 | What does a digital signature prove when spending a transaction? | That the sender controls the private key matching the funding address, authorizing the spend, without ever revealing the private key itself. Exam tip: Bitcoin and Ethereum both use ECDSA over the secp256k1 curve. |
| 7 | Correct order of the transaction lifecycle? | Build → Sign → Broadcast → Confirm (then additional confirmations accrue over time toward finality). Exam tip: exam frequently tests this exact sequence. |
| 8 | What actually determines a Bitcoin transaction's fee? | Transaction size in vBytes multiplied by the chosen fee rate (sat/vByte) — NOT the amount of BTC being sent. Exam tip: common trap is assuming fee scales with the transfer amount. |
| 9 | What is a transaction ID (txid) and its format on Bitcoin? | A 64-character hexadecimal string (32 bytes) uniquely identifying a transaction, used to look it up on a block explorer. Exam tip: pre-SegWit, txids could change before confirmation due to transaction malleability; SegWit resolved this. |
| 10 | What are the typical fee priority tiers offered by most wallet apps? | Low (slow/cheapest), Medium (standard confirmation time), High/Priority (fastest, most expensive). Exam tip: even 'high' priority fees may take longer than usual during network congestion. |
| 11 | What can a sender do if a broadcast transaction is stuck unconfirmed due to a low fee? | Use Replace-By-Fee (RBF) to rebroadcast with a higher fee, or Child-Pays-For-Parent (CPFP) to incentivize miners with a follow-up transaction. Exam tip: RBF must be signaled as available at the time the original transaction was created. |
| 12 | What is a blockchain reorg and why does it matter for confirmations? | A reorganization replaces recently mined blocks with a competing, longer chain, potentially reversing transactions that were only shallowly confirmed. Exam tip: this is why exchanges require multiple confirmations before crediting large deposits. |

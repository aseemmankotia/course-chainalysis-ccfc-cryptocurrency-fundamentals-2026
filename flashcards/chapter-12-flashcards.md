## Chapter 12: Full CCFC Exam Simulation and Time-Management Strategy — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | In a 180-min / 90-question full simulation, what is the target average time per question on the first pass? | ~2 minutes/question. Exam tip: check your pace every 15-20 questions — if you're over budget, tighten your per-question cap immediately rather than waiting until the end. |
| 2 | How much time should you reserve for final review after the first pass in a 180-minute simulation? | 10-15 minutes. Exam tip: this buffer exists specifically to revisit FLAGGED questions, not to re-read the entire exam. |
| 3 | What defines a question worth flagging vs. one worth guessing and moving on? | Flag if you've eliminated at least 2 of 4 options and remain torn between the rest. Guess-and-move-on if you have zero elimination progress — flagging those wastes review time later. |
| 4 | What is a 'cross-domain scenario question'? | A question that requires satisfying constraints from two different exam domains simultaneously (e.g., cost optimization + compliance retention). Exam tip: the last sentence of the prompt often hides the second constraint. |
| 5 | Best elimination heuristic when 3 options solve the primary requirement but fail a secondary constraint? | Isolate the secondary constraint (usually the last sentence of the prompt) and test each option against it specifically — don't just pick the first option that solves the primary ask. |
| 6 | During final review, which flagged questions should you tackle FIRST with limited time? | Questions where you've already eliminated 3 of 4 options. Highest probability of flipping to correct per minute spent — prioritize these over questions with only 1 option eliminated. |
| 7 | Why is answer length NOT a reliable signal of correctness? | Exam writers deliberately vary distractor length; short and long options are equally likely to be correct or incorrect. Never use length as an elimination heuristic. |
| 8 | After a simulation, you score 68% overall but 40% on cross-domain questions. What's the correct gap-analysis action? | Isolate the domain PAIRING causing the errors and re-study that pairing specifically — a strong overall score can mask a critical sub-category weakness that fails on the real exam. |
| 9 | What is the core difference in purpose between a chapter quiz and a full exam simulation? | Chapter quizzes test single-domain knowledge in isolation. Full simulations train time allocation, stamina, and recognition of MIXED/cross-domain scenario questions under realistic pressure. |
| 10 | Pacing check rule of thumb: at question 15 of 90, how much time should have elapsed in a 180-min exam? | ~30 minutes (15 questions x 2 min/question budget). If you're significantly over this at the 15-question mark, tighten your per-question time cap immediately. |
| 11 | What should you do with a question you have ZERO idea how to answer, even after reading twice? | Make your best guess, do NOT flag it (flagging is reserved for partially-eliminated questions), and move on immediately — re-reading with no new information wastes fixed time. |
| 12 | What's the risk of flagging every question you're even slightly unsure about? | It removes prioritization value — with too many flags, you can't tell which ones have the highest payoff for the limited review time remaining. Flag selectively (2-of-4 eliminated). |

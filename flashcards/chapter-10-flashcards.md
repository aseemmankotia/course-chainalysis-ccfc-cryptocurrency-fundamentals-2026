## Chapter 10: Stolen Funds, Hacks, Sanctions Evasion, and Money-Laundering Patterns — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | Three classic stages of money laundering? | Placement → Layering → Integration. Exam tip: mixers/chain-hopping/peel chains = layering stage; converting laundered crypto into real goods, real estate, or clean fiat = integration. |
| 2 | Chain-hopping vs. peel chain — key difference? | Chain-hopping = moving value across multiple different blockchains/asset types (bridges, DEX aggregators) to break the trace. Peel chain = sequential same-chain transactions 'peeling off' small amounts from one large UTXO into new addresses. Exam tip: both are layering, but only chain-hopping crosses chains. |
| 3 | FATF Recommendation 16 Travel Rule threshold for VASPs? | USD/EUR 1,000 — above this, originator and beneficiary information must be collected and transmitted. Exam tip: don't confuse with the traditional wire-transfer $3,000 threshold. |
| 4 | First-ever OFAC-sanctioned virtual currency mixer, and when? | Blender.io, sanctioned May 2022 — used to launder proceeds from the Ronin Bridge hack. Exam tip: Lazarus Group shifted heavily to Tornado Cash after this designation. |
| 5 | Sinbad.io — what and when sanctioned? | Bitcoin mixer sanctioned by OFAC November 29, 2023; identified as a likely successor/rebrand of Blender.io and heavily used by Lazarus Group to launder bridge-hack proceeds. |
| 6 | Tornado Cash OFAC sanctions timeline? | Designated Aug 8, 2022 → Fifth Circuit (Van Loon v. Treasury), Nov 2024, ruled immutable smart contracts aren't 'property' blockable under IEEPA → OFAC removed Tornado Cash from the SDN list March 21, 2025. Exam tip: sanctions can still apply to the humans/entities behind a mixer even after the contract itself is delisted. |
| 7 | Ronin Bridge hack — amount, date, method, attribution? | $625M, March 2022. Attacker compromised 5 of 9 validator nodes to forge fraudulent withdrawal signatures. Attributed to Lazarus Group (North Korea). |
| 8 | Bybit hack — amount, date, attribution, behavior? | ~$1.46B, February 21, 2025 — largest crypto hack on record. Attributed to Lazarus Group/TraderTraitor (North Korea). Funds showed rapid dispersal across hundreds of addresses within minutes, followed by mixing and chain-hopping. |
| 9 | Custodial mixer vs. CoinJoin (Wasabi/Samourai Whirlpool) — forensic difference? | Custodial mixer (e.g., Tornado Cash, Blender.io) pools funds and breaks linkage via a smart-contract/operator hop. CoinJoin is a trustless, single-transaction merge of many users' inputs/outputs creating output ambiguity, no custodian involved. Exam tip: Samourai Whirlpool shut down after founders' April 2024 arrest. |
| 10 | What is a 'nested service' in crypto compliance? | An unregistered broker/exchange operating through a single account at a licensed VASP, letting its own customers transact without direct KYC by the underlying VASP. Exam tip: creates correspondent-VASP-style risk — licensed exchange loses visibility into true beneficial owners. |
| 11 | Why are unhosted (self-custody) wallet transfers higher AML risk under FATF guidance? | No VASP performs KYC/Travel Rule data collection on the counterparty, so beneficial ownership can't be verified. Exam tip: many VASPs apply enhanced due diligence specifically for unhosted-wallet counterparties. |
| 12 | Typical post-hack laundering behavior in the first hours after an exploit? | Rapid dispersal — stolen funds split across hundreds of new addresses within minutes/hours (before exchanges can freeze deposits), often followed by mixing and cross-chain bridging. Exam tip: speed + address fragmentation is the signature that distinguishes exploit cash-out from routine large transfers. |

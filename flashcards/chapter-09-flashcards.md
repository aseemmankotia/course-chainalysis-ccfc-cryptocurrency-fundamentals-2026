## Chapter 9: Scams, Ransomware, and Darknet Markets — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | Rug pull — core on-chain signature? | Deployer/LP-holder wallet suddenly removes liquidity from a DEX pool after a price pump, collapsing token value near zero. Exam tip: single sudden liquidity-drain event, not recurring deposits. |
| 2 | Pig butchering scam — typical fund flow structure? | Many small victim deposits over weeks → consolidation into hub wallet → conversion to stablecoin → OTC broker/exchange off-ramp. Exam tip: driven by long-con social engineering (romance/investment chat). |
| 3 | Ponzi / high-yield investment scheme — defining fund flow? | 'Returns' paid to earlier investors are funded directly by new investor deposits, with no external revenue source. Exam tip: continuous deposit-funded payout cycle, not a single event. |
| 4 | Ransomware cash-out — key obfuscation technique tested on exam | Chain-hopping: ransom payment split and moved across multiple intermediary wallets/chains before reaching an exchange or mixer, to break the link back to the ransom demand address. |
| 5 | Rug pull vs Ponzi vs Pig butchering — key distinguishing question | Rug pull = DeFi liquidity event (single). Ponzi = deposit-funded payouts (recurring). Pig butchering = individually-targeted social engineering funnel (gradual, per-victim). Exam tip: focus on the fund-flow pattern, not the marketing narrative. |
| 6 | Darknet market escrow — standard trust mechanism | Multisig escrow (commonly 2-of-3: buyer, vendor, market) holds funds until order completion, requiring two of three keys to release. Compare to single-custody escrow, where the market alone controls release (higher exit-scam risk). |
| 7 | Finalize Early (FE) vs standard escrow release — what's the difference? | FE releases escrowed funds to the vendor before the buyer confirms order completion (higher risk, exploited in scams). Standard escrow release only occurs after buyer confirmation or dispute resolution. |
| 8 | Vendor bond — purpose on darknet markets | Refundable crypto collateral a vendor posts with the marketplace before being allowed to list products. Forfeited for rule violations. Exam tip: vendor-level trust deposit, distinct from per-transaction escrow. |
| 9 | Darknet market exit scam — key on-chain indicator | Sudden large-scale outflow of nearly all held/escrowed funds to a small number of new addresses, coinciding with the marketplace site going offline. Distinct from steady, proportional vendor payouts seen in normal operation. |
| 10 | Ransomware extortion model — single vs double extortion | Single extortion: ransom demanded only to decrypt data. Double extortion: ransom demanded to decrypt data AND to prevent public leak of exfiltrated data. Exam tip: double extortion typically raises demand amount and cash-out urgency. |
| 11 | Common on-chain indicator: 'funnel' wallet pattern | Many small inbound transactions from distinct addresses converging into one hub wallet, then a single large outbound transaction. Seen in pig butchering fund collection and Ponzi deposit aggregation. Exam tip: direction and volume asymmetry is the tell. |
| 12 | Why victim-reported cases matter for typology attribution | They ground-truth an on-chain pattern to a confirmed scam type, letting analysts validate heuristics and then apply them to similar unreported wallet clusters. Exam tip: reports supplement clustering — they don't replace it. |

## Chapter 6: Following the Flow of Funds: Pseudonymity vs Anonymity — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | Direct exposure vs indirect exposure — definition? | Direct exposure = wallet transacted in one hop with a risky entity. Indirect exposure = risky entity reached only through one or more intermediary hops. Exam tip: classification is based strictly on hop count, not on the type of entity involved. |
| 2 | What is a 'peeling chain'? | A pattern where a large UTXO is repeatedly split into a small spend output and a larger change output sent to a new address, repeated across many transactions. Used to obscure the ultimate resting place of large funds. |
| 3 | What is 'chain-hopping'? | Moving value sequentially across multiple assets and/or blockchains (e.g., BTC → ETH via DEX → bridge to another chain → privacy coin) specifically to break the continuity single-chain tracing tools depend on. |
| 4 | Monero ring signature size (post-2022 protocol upgrade)? | Fixed ring size of 16 (1 real input + 15 decoys). Exam tip: this fixed ring size, combined with RingCT and stealth addresses, defeats common-input-ownership heuristics used on transparent chains. |
| 5 | Why do cross-chain bridges break multi-hop tracing? | Most bridges lock/burn assets on the source chain and mint/release from a shared pooled contract on the destination chain — there's no direct 1:1 on-chain link between a specific deposit and withdrawal. Analysts must rely on timing and amount correlation instead. |
| 6 | Pseudonymity vs anonymity on public blockchains? | Pseudonymous (Bitcoin, Ethereum): all transaction data is public and tied to a persistent address (pseudonym) that CAN be linked to identity via clustering/KYC leaks. True anonymity: participants/amounts cannot be linked even with full ledger access — the goal of privacy coins. |
| 7 | Why do privacy coins resist standard clustering heuristics? | Ring signatures obscure the true sender among decoys, RingCT hides amounts, and stealth addresses generate a unique one-time receiving address per transaction — defeating common-input-ownership and address-reuse heuristics. |
| 8 | What compounds across a multi-hop trace involving mixing points (DEX, bridge, privacy coin)? | Confidence in the linkage degrades and compounds at each obfuscation point. Exam tip: case files must document confidence per hop, not just at the final destination. |
| 9 | Minimum elements required in a case file entry per hop? | Transaction hash, timestamp, amount, address cluster identification, and a stated confidence level. Exam tip: omitting intermediate hops for 'brevity' is a documented exam trap — it destroys the evidentiary trail. |
| 10 | Common Input Ownership Heuristic (CIOH) — what does it assume, and where does it fail? | CIOH assumes all inputs to a single transaction are controlled by the same entity, enabling address clustering. It fails against CoinJoin-style transactions and privacy coins where inputs are deliberately mixed with unrelated parties. |
| 11 | Peeling chain vs CoinJoin — key distinguishing feature? | Peeling chain: sequential large-to-small splits from a single source across many transactions. CoinJoin: multiple independent users combine inputs/outputs into one equal-value transaction round to obscure ownership mapping. |
| 12 | What is 'layering' in fund-flow analysis? | Passing funds through multiple intermediary wallets, services, or assets in successive stages specifically to increase the number of hops between source and destination, degrading traceability and diluting exposure confidence. |
